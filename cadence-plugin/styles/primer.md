# Primer template

Structure for research primers — `/research primer` writes this shape
into a substrate's `index.md`, and a graduated primer keeps it in
`wiki/primers/`. Edit freely; your version wins.

A primer is the on-ramp to **a body of sources** (the capstone tells
the story of the work — different artifact). Written so future-you can
re-enter a topic in minutes.

## Required shape

1. **Orientation (2–4 paragraphs)** — what this material covers, the
   concepts everything rests on in plain words, where the live
   tensions or open debates are. No source-by-source summary;
   synthesize.
2. **Suggested learning** — an ordered reading list over the sources,
   one line of rationale each:
   `1. [[<note-id>]] — <why this source, why at this position>`
   Order by pedagogy (what unlocks what), not by ingestion date.
3. **Open questions** *(optional)* — what the sources don't settle.
   These are backfill targets for future research, not rhetorical
   flourishes.

## Rules

- Ground every claim in the notes. The primer synthesizes; it does
  not extend.
- Name disagreements between sources plainly — "X claims A; Y's data
  points the other way" is exactly what re-entry needs.
- Keep it short enough to read in one sitting. A primer that needs
  its own primer has failed.
