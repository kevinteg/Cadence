# GC ritual: research disposition at closure

## Intent

Close-out becomes the capstoning ritual (design doc section 8): GC is prompted, never silent, and doubles as the forcing function for capturing knowledge at the moment it is most complete. Two hook points into existing machinery. First, /complete: the closing_in_on_resolution finalization menu adds capstone alongside audit / narrative / demo / validation-review. Second, /resolve: the closure ritual gains a research-disposition step — when a capstone exists, offer raw/ disposition (Delete raw/ as the proposed default since git history retains it and back-references become citation stubs; Archive to wiki/_archive/<unit>/raw/ as the conservative option; Keep when research feeds a follow-on); when no capstone exists, encourage generating one BEFORE clearing, since this is the moment the knowledge is freshest. What always survives GC: the capstone, any graduated primer, and provenance citation stubs (title + source URL + capture date). Depends on the substrate and capstone projects landing first. Design question 3 (is delete-by-default too aggressive?) gets resolved with the user during this work and encoded. Done feels like: resolving a researched unit walks the disposition flow naturally and nothing of the learned arc is lost when raw/ clears.
