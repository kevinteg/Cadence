# Capstone template

Structure for `/narrate capstone` output — the durable narrative of a
project or pursuit, promoted to `wiki/narratives/`. Edit freely; your
version wins.

A capstone tells the story of **the work** (the research primer tells
the story of the sources — different artifact). Target length:
600–1200 words. Written for re-entry months later.

## Required shape

1. **Title (H1)** — the unit's human name, not its slug.
2. **Orientation paragraph** — what this was, in two or three
   sentences a stranger could follow. The "why this existed" lives
   here, pulled from the pursuit Why / project Intent.
3. **The arc** — McAdams as prose (headings optional, sparing):
   - *What happened* — the concrete moves, in order, with the turns
     and dead ends kept in.
   - *What it meant* — interpretation; what the work was really about
     once it clarified.
   - *What shifted* — what you understand or hold differently now.
   - *What's next* — live threads, follow-on units, open questions.
4. **How it works** *(digital/hybrid units, when architecture matters)*
   — the technical shape that future-you needs to re-enter the
   system. Diagrams per `diagrams.md` when they clarify.
5. **Sources** *(when a research substrate existed)* — one line per
   source consulted, as citation stubs that outlive the raw files:
   `- <title> — <url-or-origin> (captured <YYYY-MM-DD>) · [[<note-id>]]`
   Keep the `[[note-id]]` wikilink while the distilled note exists;
   the stub text stands alone after GC clears the substrate.

## What a capstone is not

- Not a changelog. Collapse commits into moves; skip noise.
- Not a victory lap. The no-praise rule holds even at closure.
- Not exhaustive. It's the version of the story worth re-reading.
